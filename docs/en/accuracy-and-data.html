Our principle#

RTLY-Kit has no runtime dependency, so its reference data lives inside the package. Most of this data has no official machine-readable source, so we built it from public pages. This page lists those sources, says what we compared with what, and gives the date of each check. We prefer an official source whenever we can reach one.

Good to know. We looked at sources on 2026-10-07 and 2026-10-08. Banks merge, operators change ranges and moon sightings differ, so any table can age. The data is best-effort and is not an official registry. For legal or financial decisions, use an official source as well.

The two-source rule#

A bank card BIN, a Sheba bank code or a national-code prefix is included only when at least two independent sources agree. An entry found in one source, or where sources disagree, is left out. So a missing entry means "unknown" and never "invalid". A valid card can come back with getBankName() equal to null. Some community datasets share a common origin, so we treat their agreement as strong but not fully independent. We took only facts (numeric ranges and the names they map to), and copied no source text or code.

Prayer times: what each method was compared with#

The calculation is written from standard equations. The useful outside check is to compute a city and a date and compare with a timetable that the authority itself publishes. We allow the minute rounding of both sides (the tests accept plus or minus 2 minutes). We do not count aggregator websites, because they use the same published angles. All comparisons were done on 2026-10-08.

MethodCompared withResult

Sunrise and sunset agree with the NOAA Solar Calculator within one minute (12 city and date pairs). High latitudes, elevation and the Tehran Isha and Asr were checked against independent calculations and not against a published table. The Makkah Ramadan rule follows the civil date and not the Islamic day that starts at sunset. Different authorities use different angles, so pick the method your users follow.

Data tables: the check of 2026-10-08#

We compared the tables with public pages and, where we could reach them, with official documents.

  • Bank BINs. All 39 BINs appear on public pages, and no page names a different bank. No official BIN registry could be reached.
  • Sheba codes. The 19 codes in the Central Bank specification match our table. The other codes are held by at least two public pages each, and the weakest is 090.
  • Mobile prefixes. Every prefix lies inside a block that the numbering plan lists for mobile service. Number portability means a prefix never proves the current operator.

Good to know#

Good to know. (1) Hijri months for AH 1300 to 1317 come from ICU/CLDR data, which the official site cannot confirm. (2) Iranian Islamic holidays can differ from the announcement by one or two days in estimated years. (3) Bank, Sheba, operator and national-code tables come from public lists. They are consistent with each other and with the official documents named above, and they can age. (4) We found no timetable issued by the University of Islamic Sciences in Karachi, by ISNA or by the Muslim World League, and no official Tehran table with Isha and Asr. (5) A few Arabic number-word outputs await review by a native speaker.

Found a mistake? Please open an issue on GitHub and include a source. A good source is the quickest way to get a table corrected.
